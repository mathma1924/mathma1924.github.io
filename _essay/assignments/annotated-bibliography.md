I located these articles using a variety of keywords and techniques. First, I searched keywords like database, archive, digital, digital humanities often in combination with history on Google Scholar and Virgo. The results I was getting were not what I was looking for so I tried searching elsewhere. I did find out that the Journal of American History encourages reviews of digital projects, so I was able to find some projects there. I searched keywords in the main Civil War journals to see if there were any relevant articles with digital humanities work in my field. That’s where I found a couple papers that were topically close to my research. Then I discovered the Journal of Digital History and searched for any interesting projects there. Lastly, I looked in the Computational Humanities book and found some articles I liked. I also looked at the footnotes there. 
Belanger, Elizabeth. “‘A Perfect Nuisance’: Working-Class Women and Neighborhood Development in Civil War St. Louis.” The Journal of the Civil War Era, vol. 8, no. 1, 2018, 32–63. https://www.jstor.org/stable/26381502.
	Belanger performs a spatial analysis of complaints filed by women with the provost marshal in St. Louis and analyzes ethnic compositions of the neighborhoods. This project focuses on the same topic of my research, so it is good to see how other people have approached the same archival material. She offers a good example of how to use rhetorical analysis in conjugation with digital methods.

Eddins, Crystal Nicole. “Fugitivities and Futures: Black Studies in the Digital Era.” in Lauren Tilton, David Mimno and Jessica Marie Johnson, eds., Computational Humanities. University of Minnesota Press, 2024.
Glatthaar, Joseph T. “A Tale of Two Armies: The Confederate Army of Northern Virginia and the Union Army of the Potomac and Their Cultures.” Journal of the Civil War Era, vol. 6, no. 3, 2016, pp. 315–46. http://www.jstor.org/stable/26070428. 
	Glatthaar examined the demographic characteristics of the major armies of the Civil War using digital quantitative analysis. He sought to determine the economic circumstances of the major armies, so he analyzed what percentage of each sample was poor vs well-off. This article is relevant to my work topically, but also methodologically. He was able to gather a lot of data from service records and pension files to do his analysis. I don’t have access to all that information, so I’m thinking about the possibilities and usefulness of doing this type of demographic analysis.
