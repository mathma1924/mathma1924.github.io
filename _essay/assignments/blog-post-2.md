I’m interested in using digital methods to help me excavate and analyze an incoherent archive. I’m working specifically on a digitized collection of papers created and collected by the federal provost marshals. Included in this assemblage are oaths of allegiance, court records, prison paperwork, correspondence between officials, forms of all kinds, letters from citizens asking for clemency, compensation or care. 
The first step of my scholarly practice is gathering the data in a database. Digital methods are important for my research because data gathering is the first step of my scholarly practice. My research process consists of using web scrapping to get the hyperlink and transcripts of sources, while filling out fields such as themes, place, and time manually. Gathering the data allows me to distinguish interesting patterns and shapes the questions that I am asking in my research. Using a digital database offers me numerous opportunities to utilize other DH methods in the analysis stage. 
I could use geospatial analysis to analyze patterns in how people are being treated by the provost marshals in different places. Certain areas under Union occupation for longer periods have a more sustained presence in the record. Mapping would allow me to investigate patterns regarding what issues are coming up in what region and how they are being resolved. I could also track on a map who is interacting with the provost marshals. 
Different types of data visualization would offer me a way to interpret my archival evidence. I have some basic demographic information about each person, but I cannot perform in-depth analysis on that. What I can do is separate the collection by the type of document. For instance, I can create a dataset of just the oaths of allegiances. I can then analyze the race and gender of the person taking it, the time and place, and also the language of the oath as it changed over time. Another sub-collection I could make would be all of the letters from civilians asking for different things. I would have to clean up the transcriptions first, but then I could use a tool like Voyant to investigate what language was used most frequently in this correspondence. 
I am quite intrigued by what I’ve learned about critical making and would like to incorporate that into my scholarship. Materiality is becoming a greater part of my work, as I’m thinking about the body while also realizing how little physical trace there is of the people and events I’m studying. 3D making seems like a way to restore some of that lost physicality. One idea I’ve had is to print out blocks with the words that officials used to refer to people like “old devil,” and “sus character.” I would make them different lengths depending on their frequency within the sample size and then arrange them based on how they were applied to gendered and racialized subjects.
