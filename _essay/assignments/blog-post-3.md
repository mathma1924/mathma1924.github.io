Artificial intelligence poses a grave threat to the future of humanity, but there are also opportunities to use AI agents to subvert the hegemony that is being imposed on the web. Anastasia Salter and Emily Johnson argue that we should use artificial intelligence tools in creative ways for critical making.

  Salter and Johnson demonstrated how AI had a harmful impact on creativity and the open web. One of the problems with generative AI is that it is webscrapping sites and stealing data on it. Since the large language model was trained on art stolen from creators, AI can produce images that mimic this style. Showing the group the P(doom) video was a fun way to show how AI has advanced, since the graphics were generated by AI. They talked about how companies want you to become dependent on AI so they create cut companions. They also discussed the cycle of eshittification, which is where platforms get users on, abuse them, and then die. 

  Salter and Johnson argue that even though the tools are biased, we can use them in alternate ways. Salter said that digital culture and tools exemplify “toxic geek masculinity” and that coding was traditionally viewed as masculine. Part of making the internet more accessible was creating low coding tools such as Twine. Now, we can use AI to assist us in making as long as we are intentional about the process. 

  Salter and Johnson also emphasize that owning what you make is important. Johnson introduced the idea of walled gardens and how proprietary software could gatekeep or disappear. They both emphasize the important of using open source tools that rely on the internet’s native languages. Different companies are invested in closing off free space on the internet. They said that “maintenance is resistance” to the changes happening as a result of artificial intelligence. 
	One of the main ways that Salter and Johnson emphasize changing your fate is maintaining control of the process. Commercial LLMs will make auto generation the default setting, rather than requiring the machine to check in with the person. When you are working with an AI, you need to make intentional decisions, so you are in control rather than letting the machine control you. 

  Anastasia Salter argues that professors cannot afford to ignore artificial intelligence but they must engage with it. She demonstrated this at the workshop, where she showcased how agentic AI can create a video game. Salter and Johnson said that a critical maker has to reject, supplement, extend, and critique the tools they are using. Artificial intelligence can take the process out of the person’s control but it can also be used in interesting ways.
